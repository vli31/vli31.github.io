Fighting the Drift: results of the reviewer experiments

This page reports the 12 experiments planned in answer to the three reviewers of "Fighting the Drift" (transfer of linear probes across the post-training stages of a language model). It shows the state of each experiment, the validation of the probe library that all experiments share, and one section per experiment in each reviewer's order of priority.

How to read it. An experiment that is final has a full section: its finding as one sentence, one chart with a table view of the same numbers, further tables, and what the numbers rest on. An experiment with a first report that is not final shows the first takeaway of that report, marked provisional. An experiment without a report shows its state only. Every section ends with the request for that experiment as it was written, in a collapsed block. Every number is read from a result file when the page is built. Numbers marked provisional come from a first report of an experiment that is not yet final; they have not been independently verified.

Terms used on this page

TPR at 1% FPR:
share of positive test prompts scored above the threshold that 1% of negative test prompts exceed.
Realised TPR, realised FPR:
the shares of positive and of negative test prompts that a probe flags when its threshold was fixed beforehand on other prompts; for TPR at 1% FPR the threshold is taken on the test set itself.
Frozen probe:
the probe trained on the source checkpoint, applied unchanged to the target checkpoint.
Full refit:
a probe trained from scratch on the target checkpoint, on the same training prompt groups as the source probe.
Correction:
the paper's method: the frozen probe adjusted with the labelled prompt groups of a training budget at the target checkpoint. The result is the corrected probe.
Prompt group:
prompts that share their first eight normalised words; groups, not prompts, are assigned to the training side or the held-out side.
Estimation set:
held-out prompt groups set aside in each seed to fit the correction's blend weight and to choose a refit's regularisation; the rest of the held-out side is the test set. In R3-E3 it also sets the thresholds and is called the validation prompts.
Seed:
one of 8 random draws of the training prompt groups and of the estimation set; all seeds share one held-out side.
Training budget:
the number of labelled prompt groups from the target checkpoint that a method may train on.
Numeric run:
the run of 2026-11-15 that recomputed the per-prompt scores of the two headline refusal transfers, the sensitivity to repeated prompts and the bootstrap intervals (R2-E1).
Verification:
a second, independent recomputation of an experiment's numbers with separate code; an experiment is final only after it.

R1-E1 Estimation-set size and equal total labelling budgets

Verifying

This experiment is recorded as verifying. A first report exists (last changed 2026-11-15 20:46 PST); it is not recorded as final, so every number in it is provisional and has not been independently verified.

provisional First takeaway of that report: The correction does not need 4,000 estimation prompts. With 500 or 1,000 it performs the same to within 0.03 points of AUROC; with 125 it becomes unreliable.

provisional Rest of the first takeaway, quoted: With the training prompts held fixed (m = 500 or 2,000; three transfers), replacing the 4,000-prompt estimation set by 1,000 prompts changes the correction's test AUROC by -0.017 to +0.000 points and by 500 prompts by -0.029 to -0.012 points (TPR at 1% FPR: -0.36 to +0.03 points). With 125 prompts the mean change is -0.254 to -0.075 points of AUROC and -3.34 to -0.43 points of TPR, but 0 of the 6 AUROC intervals exclude zero: the loss comes from a minority of seeds in which the blend weight chosen on about 130 to 650 exchanges is far from its usual value. Support: measured, 8 seeds x 6 settings; section 2, Figure 1a. Confidence: high for 500 and 1,000; the size of the loss at 125 is uncertain.

Test sensitivity to estimation-set size and compare methods at equal total labelling budgets (Q3, Weakness 1)

Use three OLMo-3 transfers: Base → Think-SFT refusal, Think-SFT → Instruct-SFT refusal, and Base → DPO harm. Compare the frozen probe, correction on the frozen probe, and a from-scratch refit.

First, fix the training sample at m=500 and m=2,000 prompt groups. At each m, vary the labelled estimation set |E| over 125, 500, 1,000, and 4,000 prompt groups. Use the same training prompts, nested estimation subsets, and seeds across methods. Recompute the correction's orthogonalisation and blend weight, and retune the refit's regularisation, at each estimation-set size.

Second, fix the total budget B=m+|E| at 2,000, 4,500, 6,000, and 8,000 prompt groups. Give both methods the same pool of B prompts. For each listed |E|<B, set m=B-|E| and report that split. Include a refit that selects regularisation by prompt-grouped cross-validation within B and then trains on all B prompts. Report all prespecified splits. If the cost table needs one split per method, choose it in advance or select it by prompt-grouped cross-validation entirely within the total budget B. Count every labelled prompt used for selection towards B.

Show the fixed-m and fixed-B results separately. Report held-out AUROC and TPR at 1% FPR in absolute units, alongside the share of headroom closed. Use the same full-data target refit as the reference throughout each transfer.

Use the existing refusal checkpoint pairs, including the low-headroom DPO and RL transfers. At each budget, keep the m training prompts fixed and divide E into folds by prompt group.   • Variation between seeds is not in these intervals. The rule is exact text after removing the wrapper; paraphrases and partial overlaps are not counted.
