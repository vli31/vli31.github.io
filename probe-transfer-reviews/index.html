Terms used on this page

TPR at 1% FPR:
share of positive test prompts scored above the threshold that 1% of negative test prompts exceed.
Realised TPR, realised FPR:
the shares of positive and of negative test prompts that a probe flags when its threshold was fixed beforehand on other prompts; for TPR at 1% FPR the threshold is taken on the test set itself.
Frozen probe:
the probe trained on the source checkpoint, applied unchanged to the target checkpoint.
Full refit:
a probe trained from scratch on the target checkpoint, on the same training prompt groups as the source probe.
Correction:
the paper's method: the frozen probe adjusted with the labelled prompt groups of a training budget at the target checkpoint. The result is the corrected probe.
Prompt group:
prompts that share their first eight normalised words; groups, not prompts, are assigned to the training side or the held-out side.
Estimation set:
held-out prompt groups set aside in each seed to fit the correction's blend weight and to choose a refit's regularisation; the rest of the held-out side is the test set. In R3-E3 it also sets the thresholds and is called the validation prompts.
Seed:
one of 8 random draws of the training prompt groups and of the estimation set; all seeds share one held-out side.
Training budget:
the number of labelled prompt groups from the target checkpoint that a method may train on.
Numeric run:
the run that recomputed the per-prompt scores of the two headline refusal transfers, the sensitivity to repeated prompts and the bootstrap intervals (R2-E1).
Verification:
a second, independent recomputation of an experiment's numbers with separate code; an experiment is final only after it.

Takeaways of the report (verbatim)

Quoted from the report of R1-E1; the sections, tables and figures it names are those of the report. Names on a tinted background are identifiers of the code and of the data files, not terms of this page.

In the report a "prompt" is a prompt group, m is the number of training prompt groups, |E| the number of estimation prompt groups and B = m + |E| their total.

  1. The correction does not need 4,000 estimation prompts. With 500 or 1,000 its mean AUROC stays within 0.03 points of its value with 4,000; with 125 it becomes unreliable. With the training prompts held fixed (m = 500 or 2,000; three transfers), replacing the 4,000-prompt estimation set by 1,000 prompts changes the correction's test AUROC by -0.017 to +0.000 points (the 95% intervals reach -0.034; 2 of 6 exclude zero, 0 after the Holm adjustment) and by 500 prompts by -0.029 to -0.012 points (intervals reach -0.058; 3 of 6 exclude zero, 0 after the Holm adjustment). TPR at 1% FPR changes by -0.36 to +0.03 points. With 125 prompts the mean change is -0.254 to -0.075 points of AUROC and -3.34 to -0.43 points of TPR, and 0 of the 6 AUROC intervals exclude zero: the loss sits in a minority of seeds. A post-hoc diagnostic separates two causes. All 8 blend weights above 2 (all with 125 estimation prompts, all on refusal) sit at the upper bound of the paper's bounded search, on one of its probe points or next to one, and in 7 of these 8 cells the search did not return the weight that maximises its own criterion. With the same criterion maximised by an exhaustive sweep the mean loss at 125 is still -0.176 to -0.064 points, and 10 of 48 seed x setting results lose more than 0.1 points (14 with the paper's search). Support: measured, 8 seeds x 6 settings; section 2, Figure 1, left. Confidence: high that 500 and 1,000 prompts cost at most a few hundredths of a point; the size of the loss at 125 is uncertain.
  2. On the two refusal transfers the correction has no advantage over a refit that gets the same total number of labelled prompts. At the split fixed in advance (|E| = 500 at B = 2,000, |E| = 1,000 at B = 4,500, 6,000 and 8,000), correction minus the refit trained on the same split is -0.009 to +0.008 points of AUROC, and 0 of the 8 intervals exclude zero. A refit that uses all B prompts for training and picks its regularisation by cross-validation inside them is higher than the correction in 8 of 8 comparisons, by 0.017 to 0.062 points of AUROC (6 of 8 with a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05) and 0.15 to 0.97 points of TPR. As a share of the AUROC headroom the differences are -1.0 to -0.3 points. The interval over seeds excludes zero in 6 of the 8 comparisons, and a bootstrap over test prompts (post hoc) puts the 95% interval of the seed-mean AUROC difference below zero in 8 of 8. Support: measured; section 3, section 5, Figure 1, right. Confidence: high that the correction has no advantage; the refit's lead is small (a few hundredths of a point) but consistent (62 of 64 seed x budget results).
  3. On harm (Base to DPO) the correction is ahead of both refits at every budget. At the pre-specified split it is +0.279 to +0.834 points of AUROC and +1.62 to +5.01 points of TPR above the cross-validated refit on the same B prompts (higher in 32 of 32 seed x budget results; the prompt-bootstrap intervals are above zero in 4 of 4). Over all 15 splits its AUROC is above the cross-validated refit in 119 of 120 seed x split results, and the interval over seeds excludes zero in 14 of 15 splits; the exception is |E| = 125 at B = 8,000, +0.138 [-0.074, +0.351] points, 7 of 8 seeds. The correction closes 40.7, 61.9, 66.3, 70.0% of the AUROC headroom at B = 2,000, 4,500, 6,000 and 8,000; the cross-validated refit closes -101.7, -23.9, 0.5, 22.3%, that is, it is below the frozen probe at B = 2,000 and 4,500 and level with it at 6,000. Support: measured; section 3. Confidence: high.
  4. For a fixed budget the split matters more than the method on refusal: 500 to 1,000 estimation prompts and the rest for training is better for both methods than 4,000 estimation prompts. Among the splits with 500 or more estimation prompts, the paper's split (|E| = 4,000) gives the lowest AUROC in 18 of 18 cases (3 transfers x B = 4,500, 6,000, 8,000 x 2 methods). The pre-specified split is above the paper's split in every seed in 18 of these 18 cases, by +0.053 to +0.319 points of AUROC for the correction and +0.065 to +1.323 for the refit (18 of 18 intervals exclude zero). Example, Base to Think-SFT, B = 4,500: the correction reaches 98.90 ± 0.03% AUROC with 500 training + 4,000 estimation prompts and 99.17 ± 0.02% with 3,500 + 1,000. Support: measured; section 3. Confidence: high.
  5. Limits. Three transfers, one layer, one model family. The intervals in the tables are over 8 seeds that share most of their test prompts; the test-set error is estimated separately (post hoc) and no interval combines the two. |E| below 125, |E| between 125 and 500, and budgets below 2,000 were not run. The two concepts differ in several ways at once (headroom, exchanges per prompt, label source), so the harm against refusal contrast cannot be attributed to one cause.

R1-E4 Stage order and training duration from existing checkpoints

Final

From the same Think-SFT checkpoint and with the same refusal probe, the frozen probe's AUROC is 0.9295 after a further SFT stage (Instruct-SFT) and 0.9930 after a DPO stage (Think-DPO), against 0.9938 at Think-SFT itself; a full refit reaches 0.9936 and 0.9945, so the gap between frozen probe and full refit is 0.0640 and 0.0015.

  • The gap in two parts. Frozen loss is how far the frozen probe falls below its own AUROC at the source checkpoint; ceiling drift is how far the full refit lies above that same level. Instruct-SFT: gap +0.0640 = frozen loss +0.0642 + ceiling drift −0.0002 (not distinguishable from zero: at least one of its two 95% intervals includes zero). Think-DPO: gap +0.0015 = frozen loss +0.0008 + ceiling drift +0.0007.
  • TPR at 1% FPR: the frozen probe has 0.4693 at Instruct-SFT and 0.8525 at Think-DPO, against 0.8679 at Think-SFT; the full refit has 0.8875 and 0.8807.
  • The labels change with the checkpoint, because refusal is judged on each checkpoint's own responses: 8.36 to 8.51% of the test prompts change label between Think-SFT and Instruct-SFT and 2.08 to 2.27% between Think-SFT and Think-DPO. The frozen loss therefore mixes a change of labels with a change of the probe's scores. With the labels held fixed in three ways, the frozen loss at Instruct-SFT is 0.0315 to 0.0427 instead of 0.0642.
  • Training duration (harm; the same Base probe, training prompt groups, estimation set and test prompts at every checkpoint of the Think-SFT run): the frozen probe's AUROC is 0.9636 at step 15,000, 0.9622 at step 30,000 and 0.9623 at step 43,000 (final), against 0.9655 at Base; the full refit is 0.9685 to 0.9688; the correction with 3,200 training prompt groups is 0.9657 to 0.9661.
  • No attribution is possible: the two stages differ at once in objective, learning rate, data, system prompt, the token at which the activation is read and the share of labels that change. The experiment describes the two stages and attributes the gap to none of these. Nothing in it measures gradient density.

On Base to Think-SFT refusal, with its source threshold the frozen probe flags no Think-SFT test prompt in any of the 8 seeds; with the threshold recalibrated on the target its realised TPR is 0.420 at a realised FPR of 1.03%; the corrected probe with the same recalibration reaches 0.794 (500 training prompt groups) and 0.827 (2,000) at 1.08% and 1.06%.

  • What recalibration alone recovers (condition 2 against condition 1): the realised TPR goes from 0.000 to 0.420 (95% paired-t interval of the change over the 8 seeds 0.382 to 0.457) and the realised FPR from 0.00% to 1.03%. The two conditions are not at a common FPR: this is what the recalibrated threshold yields, not a gain at a fixed FPR.
  • The paper's value for the frozen probe, a TPR at 1% FPR of 0.416, takes its threshold from the target test set. It is therefore the situation of condition 2 (a threshold already recalibrated on the target), not that of a probe used with its source threshold.
  • Why the source threshold flags nothing: the threshold (mean 1.06 logits) lies above the frozen probe's largest score on the Think-SFT test prompts in every seed, by 0.14 to 2.06 logits. The frozen probe's median score of refused test prompts is 6.2 logits lower at Think-SFT than at Base (−3.95 against +2.26); for non-refused prompts it moves by 0.25 (−6.25 against −6.50).
  • With 500 instead of 4,000 validation prompt groups every threshold rests on 3 or 4 non-refused validation prompts that score above it. The seed means stay close (realised TPR 0.396, 0.811 and 0.826 for conditions 2 and 3), but single seeds spread widely: the realised FPR of the recalibrated frozen probe ranges from 0.20% to 3.37%.

What these numbers rest on, and what they cannot show

  • Check against the paper's stored values: the TPR at 1% FPR of the frozen probe and of the two corrected probes equals the stored per-seed value in 24 of 24 comparisons (largest absolute difference 0).
  • The threshold rule is the same in every condition: the 0.99 quantile of the scores of the validation prompts that are not refused (an observed score); a test prompt is flagged when its score is strictly above the threshold. The frozen probe never saw validation or test prompts.
  • One transfer, one concept and one layer: Base to Think-SFT, refusal, layer 16. The size of the effects for other transfers, for harm or for other layers was not measured.
  • Training and prompt format are confounded: Base reads the prompt as plain text and Think-SFT through a chat template, and the activation is taken at a different final token, so the shift of the frozen probe's scores can come from either.
  • Condition 1 depends on how the frozen probe is defined. The paper's frozen probe keeps the Base standardisation of the activations. Re-standardised with Think-SFT activation statistics, which needs no label, the frozen probe with the source threshold reaches a realised TPR of 0.217 at a realised FPR of 0.39% (a row that was not pre-registered).
  • Only one way of changing the scoring direction was compared with the frozen probe. The paper's stored refit from scratch on the same training prompt groups has about the same TPR at 1% FPR at these sizes: 0.789 with 500 training prompt groups and 0.827 with 2,000 (values quoted in the report, not recomputed in this experiment), against 0.790 and 0.826 for the corrected probe. This experiment does not rank the correction against that refit.
  • Condition 3 uses the validation prompts twice, for the two coefficients of the correction and for the threshold. Its realised FPR is not distinguishable from that of condition 2, but its spread over seeds is larger, and 8 seeds cannot say why.
  • No interval covers every source of variation at once: the SE and the paired-t interval over seeds treat the seeds as independent although they share test prompts, and the bootstrap resamples prompts with the probes held fixed.
  • The blend weight of the correction can move with numerical details: it comes from a bounded search on AUROC, which is a step function of the weight. A second implementation, written for the verification, selects another weight in 2 of 32 corrected probes; in one of them the TPR at 1% FPR on the test set is 0.7960 instead of 0.7952, inside the design tolerance of 0.005.
  • A label is the majority over sampled responses as judged by an LLM judge. Label noise enters the thresholds and the rates and was not modelled.
  • Only the 1% target was run.
