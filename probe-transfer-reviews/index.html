Terms used on this page

TPR at 1% FPR:
share of positive test prompts scored above the threshold that 1% of negative test prompts exceed.
Realised TPR, realised FPR:
the shares of positive and of negative test prompts that a probe flags when its threshold was fixed beforehand on other prompts; for TPR at 1% FPR the threshold is taken on the test set itself.
Frozen probe:
the probe trained on the source checkpoint, applied unchanged to the target checkpoint.
Full refit:
a probe trained from scratch on the target checkpoint, on the same training prompt groups as the source probe.
Correction:
the paper's method: the frozen probe adjusted with the labelled prompt groups of a training budget at the target checkpoint. The result is the corrected probe.
Starting probe:
in R3-E1, a probe that needs no label from the target checkpoint: the frozen probe, or the frozen probe moved through a map between the activations of the two checkpoints that is fitted without labels. A Procrustes map is an orthogonal map (a rotation); a ridge map is a regularised linear regression. The correction can start from any of them.
Prompt group:
prompts that share their first eight normalised words; groups, not prompts, are assigned to the training side or the held-out side.
Estimation set:
held-out prompt groups set aside in each seed to fit the correction's blend weight and to choose a refit's regularisation; the rest of the held-out side is the test set. In R3-E3 it also sets the thresholds and is called the validation prompts.
Seed:
one of 8 random draws of the training prompt groups and of the estimation set; all seeds share one held-out side.
Training budget:
the number of labelled prompt groups from the target checkpoint that a method may train on.
Total budget:
in R1-E1 and R1-E2, all labelled prompt groups from the target checkpoint that a method may use, for training and for its estimation set together.
Headroom:
the gap between the full refit and the frozen probe on the same test set (full refit minus frozen probe): the most that a method can gain over leaving the probe unchanged.
Numeric run:
the run that recomputed the per-prompt scores of the two headline refusal transfers, the sensitivity to repeated prompts and the bootstrap intervals (R2-E1).
Verification:
a second, independent recomputation of an experiment's numbers with separate code; an experiment is final only after it.

Takeaways of the report (verbatim)

Quoted from the report of R1-E1; the sections, tables and figures it names are those of the report.

In the report a "prompt" is a prompt group, m is the number of training prompt groups, |E| the number of estimation prompt groups and B = m + |E| their total.

  1. The correction does not need 4,000 estimation prompts. With 500 or 1,000 its mean AUROC stays within 0.03 points of its value with 4,000; with 125 it becomes unreliable. With the training prompts held fixed (m = 500 or 2,000; three transfers), replacing the 4,000-prompt estimation set by 1,000 prompts changes the correction's test AUROC by -0.017 to +0.000 points (the 95% intervals reach -0.034; 2 of 6 exclude zero, 0 after the Holm adjustment) and by 500 prompts by -0.029 to -0.012 points (intervals reach -0.058; 3 of 6 exclude zero, 0 after the Holm adjustment). TPR at 1% FPR changes by -0.36 to +0.03 points. With 125 prompts the mean change is -0.254 to -0.075 points of AUROC and -3.34 to -0.43 points of TPR, and 0 of the 6 AUROC intervals exclude zero: the loss sits in a minority of seeds. A post-hoc diagnostic separates two causes. All 8 blend weights above 2 (all with 125 estimation prompts, all on refusal) sit at the upper bound of the paper's bounded search, on one of its probe points or next to one, and in 7 of these 8 cells the search did not return the weight that maximises its own criterion. With the same criterion maximised by an exhaustive sweep the mean loss at 125 is still -0.176 to -0.064 points, and 10 of 48 seed x setting results lose more than 0.1 points (14 with the paper's search). Support: measured, 8 seeds x 6 settings; section 2, Figure 1, left. Confidence: high that 500 and 1,000 prompts cost at most a few hundredths of a point; the size of the loss at 125 is uncertain.
  2. On the two refusal transfers the correction has no advantage over a refit that gets the same total number of labelled prompts. At the split fixed in advance (|E| = 500 at B = 2,000, |E| = 1,000 at B = 4,500, 6,000 and 8,000), correction minus the refit trained on the same split is -0.009 to +0.008 points of AUROC, and 0 of the 8 intervals exclude zero. A refit that uses all B prompts for training and picks its regularisation by cross-validation inside them is higher than the correction in 8 of 8 comparisons, by 0.017 to 0.062 points of AUROC (6 of 8 with a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05) and 0.15 to 0.97 points of TPR. As a share of the AUROC headroom the differences are -1.0 to -0.3 points. The interval over seeds excludes zero in 6 of the 8 comparisons, and a bootstrap over test prompts (post hoc) puts the 95% interval of the seed-mean AUROC difference below zero in 8 of 8. Support: measured; section 3, section 5, Figure 1, right. Confidence: high that the correction has no advantage; the refit's lead is small (a few hundredths of a point) but consistent (62 of 64 seed x budget results).
  3. On harm (Base to DPO) the correction is ahead of both refits at every budget. At the pre-specified split it is +0.279 to +0.834 points of AUROC and +1.62 to +5.01 points of TPR above the cross-validated refit on the same B prompts (higher in 32 of 32 seed x budget results; the prompt-bootstrap intervals are above zero in 4 of 4). Over all 15 splits its AUROC is above the cross-validated refit in 119 of 120 seed x split results, and the interval over seeds excludes zero in 14 of 15 splits; the exception is |E| = 125 at B = 8,000, +0.138 [-0.074, +0.351] points, 7 of 8 seeds. The correction closes 40.7, 61.9, 66.3, 70.0% of the AUROC headroom at B = 2,000, 4,500, 6,000 and 8,000; the cross-validated refit closes -101.7, -23.9, 0.5, 22.3%, that is, it is below the frozen probe at B = 2,000 and 4,500 and level with it at 6,000. Support: measured; section 3. Confidence: high.
  4. For a fixed budget the split matters more than the method on refusal: 500 to 1,000 estimation prompts and the rest for training is better for both methods than 4,000 estimation prompts. Among the splits with 500 or more estimation prompts, the paper's split (|E| = 4,000) gives the lowest AUROC in 18 of 18 cases (3 transfers x B = 4,500, 6,000, 8,000 x 2 methods). The pre-specified split is above the paper's split in every seed in 18 of these 18 cases, by +0.053 to +0.319 points of AUROC for the correction and +0.065 to +1.323 for the refit (18 of 18 intervals exclude zero). Example, Base to Think-SFT, B = 4,500: the correction reaches 98.90 ± 0.03% AUROC with 500 training + 4,000 estimation prompts and 99.17 ± 0.02% with 3,500 + 1,000. Support: measured; section 3. Confidence: high.
  5. Limits. Three transfers, one layer, one model family. The intervals in the tables are over 8 seeds that share most of their test prompts; the test-set error is estimated separately (post hoc) and no interval combines the two. |E| below 125, |E| between 125 and 500, and budgets below 2,000 were not run. The two concepts differ in several ways at once (headroom, exchanges per prompt, label source), so the harm against refusal contrast cannot be attributed to one cause.

Test sensitivity to estimation-set size and compare methods at equal total labelling budgets (Q3, Weakness 1)

Use three OLMo-3 transfers: Base → Think-SFT refusal, Think-SFT → Instruct-SFT refusal, and Base → DPO harm. Compare the frozen probe, correction on the frozen probe, and a from-scratch refit.

First, fix the training sample at m=500 and m=2,000 prompt groups. At each m, vary the labelled estimation set |E| over 125, 500, 1,000, and 4,000 prompt groups. Second, fix the total budget B=m+|E| at 2,000, 4,500, 6,000, and 8,000 prompt groups. Give both methods the same pool of B prompts. For each listed |E|<B, set m=B-|E| and report that split. R1-E2 Practical cost of reaching the same performance

Final

On the two refusal transfers the correction reaches neither 90% nor 95% of the full-refit AUROC headroom with fewer labelled prompt groups than a refit (all three methods reach 90% at 2,000 labelled prompt groups, the smallest total budget tested; for 95% on Base to Think-SFT the correction needs 6,000 and a refit cross-validated inside the total budget 4,500); on harm (Base to DPO) the order is the correction on the ridge-mapped probe (22,200 for 90% and 29,480 for 95%), the refit cross-validated inside the total budget (29,480 for 90% and 36,760 for 95%), the correction on the frozen probe (36,760 for both) and the refit with its regularisation tuned on the estimation set (36,760 for 90% and 40,400 for 95%), and this order is better determined than any single one of these total budgets.

  • What is counted. A total budget is a number of labelled prompt groups at the target checkpoint: training plus estimation prompt groups. The share of the headroom that a method closes is 100 x (method − frozen probe) / (full refit − frozen probe) in test AUROC, each term a mean over the 8 seeds. A method reaches 90% or 95% at the first tested total budget at which this share is at least that large; nothing is interpolated between tested budgets. Tested: 2,000, 4,500, 6,000, 8,000, 12,000, 19,000 and 24,000 prompt groups on refusal, and 12 total budgets from 2,000 to 40,400 on harm. Every method was run with all 8 seeds at every tested total budget of its transfer, the largest included. The corrections and the refit tuned on the estimation set split a total budget into training and estimation prompt groups: 500 estimation prompt groups at a total of 2,000, 1,000 estimation prompt groups at a total of 4,500, 6,000 and 8,000, and 4,000, as in the paper, from 12,000 up.
  • The methods. Correction on the frozen probe: the paper's correction. Correction on the ridge-mapped probe (harm only): the same correction, started from the frozen probe passed through a ridge map from the activations of the source checkpoint to those of the target checkpoint; the map is fitted without labels on the training prompt groups of the total budget. Refit with its regularisation tuned on the estimation set: a probe trained from scratch on the training prompt groups, the paper's refit. Refit cross-validated inside the total budget (cross-validated refit): a probe trained from scratch on all prompt groups of the total budget, its regularisation chosen by 5-fold cross-validation inside them; it has no estimation set. The full refit, which defines 100%, is trained on all 20,000 (refusal) or 36,400 (harm) training prompt groups.
  • Refusal. At 2,000 prompt groups the 6 shares (3 methods on 2 transfers) are 90.8% to 96.7%. For 95% on Base to Think-SFT the correction and the refit tuned on the estimation set both need 6,000; the cross-validated refit is at 95.2% at 4,500 (seed SD 0.3 points, test-set SD 0.4), where the correction is at 94.3%. With the seeds and the test prompts resampled together (a bootstrap that was added after the results had been seen), the cross-validated refit reaches 95% at an earlier total budget than the correction in 70.7% of replicates, at the same one in 29.3% and later in 0.0%. At 4,500 its share is 1.0 points above the correction's (2.5th to 97.5th percentile over the same replicates, a descriptive interval: +0.5 to +1.5). On Think-SFT to Instruct-SFT all three methods are above 95% at 2,000, so the tested budgets cannot rank them there.
  • Harm. With the seeds and the test prompts resampled together (a bootstrap that was added after the results had been seen), the correction on the ridge-mapped probe reaches 90% before each of the other three methods in at least 94.7% of replicates (read pair by pair; no table holds the share in which it is before all of them at once); for 95% it is before the cross-validated refit in 65.1% (same total budget 30.3%, later 4.6%). The paper's correction on the frozen probe reaches 90% after the cross-validated refit in 82.2% of replicates (same total budget 17.2%, earlier 0.6%); for 95% the two are at the same total budget in 43.3% and the refit is earlier in 56.7%.
  • What a total budget requires at the target checkpoint. A refusal prompt group holds 1.10 prompt texts on average, and each prompt text gets one label; a harm prompt group is a prompt with all its stored exchanges (4.88 per prompt group on average); an exchange is a stored text with its own label, and one activation is read from it (the mean over all its tokens). Refusal: every labelled prompt text needs 8 generated responses and each response is judged once, whatever the method, so the total budget alone sets the labelling: 17,427 generated and as many judged responses at 2,000 prompt groups, 39,692 at 4,500 and 52,608 at 6,000, against 175,926 for the full refit. Harm: the labels exist already and are reused, so no method needs a new label. As the methods were run, each standardises the activations with the mean and the standard deviation over all 36,400 training prompt groups and therefore extracts the activations of the same 197,500 exchanges at every tested total budget from 4,500 up (5.1 to 8.9 GPU-hours, derived; hours of one GPU of the kind in the logs that they are derived from). An earlier total budget on harm therefore saves reused labels (108,692 exchanges at the earliest one against 179,766 for the paper's correction), not extraction; whether the methods keep their performance when the standardisation uses the labelled prompt groups only was not tested.
  • Two kinds of spread. Seed SD: the standard deviation of a share over 1,000 bootstrap draws of the 8 seeds; two seeds share 84.4% to 84.8% of their test prompt groups on refusal and 74.5% to 75.3% on harm, so it does not contain the sampling error of the test set. Test-set SD: the standard deviation of a share over 2,000 replicates in which whole test prompt groups are resampled; this bootstrap was added after the results had been seen. On harm from 19,000 prompt groups up the test-set SD is 0.9 to 3.8 points and is larger than the seed SD (0.4 to 2.1) in 24 of the 24 pairs of a method and a total budget that have saved per-prompt scores. Of the 20 first total budgets, 5 have a share less than one test-set SD above their target, and on harm the first total budget of a replicate is the one given here in at most 85.2% of the replicates with seeds and test prompts resampled (29.8% to 85.2%, by method and target).
  • Scale. The headroom is 3.61 points of AUROC on Base to Think-SFT (frozen probe 95.77%, full refit 99.38%), 6.40 points of AUROC on Think-SFT to Instruct-SFT (frozen probe 92.95%, full refit 99.36%) and 0.59 points of AUROC on Base to DPO (frozen probe 96.36%, full refit 96.95%); on harm the step from 90% to 95% is 0.03 points of AUROC (computed from the headroom).

What these numbers rest on, and what they cannot show

  • Every share is computed from means over the 8 seeds; a seed redraws the training prompt groups and the estimation set. The bootstrap over test prompt groups resamples whole prompt groups of the one held-out side (26,074 groups on refusal, 15,910 on harm) and leaves the training and estimation prompt groups as they are; it measures the sampling error of that side, not a shift to other prompts.
  • Where the 90 means of the tables (one method at one total budget) come from: 36 come from cells of R1-E1, 36 are the paper's stored values and 18 come from cells computed for this experiment. Up to 8,000 prompt groups the correction on the frozen probe and both refits come from R1-E1 and the correction on the ridge-mapped probe was computed here; above 8,000 the two corrections and the refit tuned on the estimation set are the paper's stored values and the cross-validated refit was computed here. In cells: this experiment computed 148, of which 112 for the cross-validated refit and 32 for the correction on the ridge-mapped probe (one transfer, one total budget and one seed each; the 18 means x 8 seeds) and 4 that repeat a cell of the paper and enter the checks only; it read 192 cells of R1-E1, one for each transfer, total budget up to 8,000, seed and kind of fit (the split of the total budget, which holds the correction and the refit tuned on the estimation set, or the cross-validated refit).
  • Checks of the numbers: 47 of 47 pass. Against the paper's stored values, where equality in every digit is expected, all 832 compared per-seed values (18 checks) equal the stored ones: 400 of the frozen probe and of the full refit, as the cells of R1-E1 hold them, at every stored training budget and seed (these two probes were read from stored fits and not fitted again in this experiment); 144 of cells of R1-E1 that repeat a cell of the paper, with the paper's split; 288 of cells of R3-E1, used here as a second check of the stored values. 26 checks compare cells that were computed twice and allow the tolerance of the design; the largest difference in them is 3.5 × 10⁻⁵ AUROC.
  • Added after the results and the verification were known: the bootstrap over test prompt groups, the recomputation of 64 of the paper's harm cells to obtain per-prompt scores (the stored values have none; the recomputed cells agree with them within the tolerance of the design and are not the source of a value in a table), and the generation time for each target checkpoint. The report states that none of them changes a first total budget.
  • In a replicate the first total budget is read on the tested budgets for which per-prompt scores of all 8 seeds were saved. Without such scores: refusal, correction on the frozen probe: 12,000, 19,000 and 24,000; refusal, refit tuned on the estimation set: 12,000, 19,000 and 24,000; harm, correction on the ridge-mapped probe: 40,400; harm, refit tuned on the estimation set: 12,000 to 25,840. On harm the shares of the refit with its regularisation tuned on the estimation set at its total budgets without scores are 15.2% to 71.6%, below both targets. (Shares: the collapsed table above.)
  • Nothing was generated, judged or extracted for this experiment: all activations of both checkpoints and all labels existed already. The GPU-hours are therefore derived, by multiplying measured counts with times per unit from logs of other checkpoints of the same model; they are a range, not a measurement at the three target checkpoints.
  • Each generated response is counted as judged once. Responses that the first judge declined were also sent to a second judge; those second calls are not counted. The report gives the judging as a count of responses only: it states no amount of money for the labelling.
  • Below 2,000 prompt groups the tested budgets do not rank the methods on refusal. Cells of R1-E1 with 500 training prompt groups, which are not among the tested total budgets (totals of 500 to 1,500 prompt groups): on Base to Think-SFT no method reaches 90% (79.7% to 86.5%); on Think-SFT to Instruct-SFT every method is at 91.3% to 92.6%, so 90% is already reached with 500 to 625 prompt groups in total, depending on the method.
  • Scope: three transfers, layer 16, one model family, and one split of each total budget into training and estimation prompt groups (500, 1,000 or 4,000 estimation prompt groups, by total budget), fixed in advance. A first total budget is read on 7 (refusal) or 12 (harm) tested budgets, so it is known to one step of that list at best.

Every first total budget on harm lies above 8,000, and every one on refusal at or below it.
What this experiment cannot show, from the report (quoted, with 1 item on money left out)

Quoted from the report of R1-E2; the sections it names are those of the report. In it a "prompt" is a prompt group; E is the estimation set and |E| its number of prompt groups; N is the number of all training prompt groups, and the all-N moments are the mean and the standard deviation of the activations over all of them, with which every method standardises the activations; a crossing is the first tested total budget at which a method reaches a target; the grid is the list of tested total budgets; the estimation region is the part of the held-out side that is set aside for the estimation sets and is in no test set; train-side prompts are prompt groups of the training side, on which the frozen probe was trained; mixed training prompts are training prompt groups of which a part comes from the estimation region and a part from the training side; the first regime is the total budgets up to 8,000 and the second regime those above. The item that is left out says that the report states no amount of money for the labelling; the list above this block says so in the page's words.

  • Below 2,000 prompts on refusal. All methods meet 90% at the smallest tested budget on both refusal transfers (and 95% on one), so the first budget at which each would cross is unknown.
  • An independent test set. The test-set bootstrap resamples the prompts of one held-out side; the eight seeds' test sets are subsets of it. It measures sampling error of that side, not a shift to other prompts. Training-set and estimation-set sampling enter only through the 8 seeds.
  • Other splits. Crossings depend on |E| (section 7). The grid uses one split per budget, fixed in advance.
  • Where the training prompts come from.   • What is compared. Every variant deploys a blend of the same two scores: the score of the prior (the frozen probe, standardised again with the activations of the target checkpoint) and the score of a probe fitted on the training budget at the target checkpoint. The angle of a blend says how the two are mixed: 0 degrees is the prior alone, 90 degrees the target-fitted probe alone, and above 90 degrees the prior's score enters with a negative coefficient. Every variant chooses its blend by AUROC on the estimation set and is scored on the same test set. The paper's rule removes from the target-fitted probe its part along the prior (orthogonalisation), with a slope estimated after centring the two scores within each class, and finds the weight of what remains with a bounded one-dimensional search. The alternatives change one thing at a time: a dense sweep of 2,001 weights instead of the bounded search, global instead of within-class centring, or no orthogonalisation at all (a direct search over the angle of the blend, from 0 to 90 or from 0 to 180 degrees).

Test whether within-class orthogonalisation improves on direct score blending

Reuse the same prior and target-fitted probe in every variant. First verify Eq. 5 numerically. Let u be the normalised prior score, v the target-fitted score, and c = alpha / ‖g_hat − beta × p‖. R3-E1 Does supervised correction add value after Procrustes alignment

Final

With training budgets of 500 to 8,000 prompt groups (4,500 to 12,000 labelled prompt groups in total), the correction adds 18.5 to 26.3 points of TPR at 1% FPR on top of a Procrustes-mapped probe on Base to Think-SFT (refusal), where the corrected probe is then no better than a refit on the same labelled prompt groups (−0.6 to −0.1 points), and 0.7 to 1.4 points on Base to DPO (harm), where the corrected probe is 2.1 to 11.4 points above that refit; one further harm value, 1.8 points, comes from a map that its data do not fully determine, and is 1.0 points with the undetermined part of the map left out.

  • What is compared. A starting probe uses no label from the target checkpoint: it is the frozen probe, or the frozen probe moved through a map between the activations of the two checkpoints that is fitted without labels (an orthogonal Procrustes map or a ridge regression; fitted on the prompts of the training budget, or on all prompt groups of the training side, 20,000 for refusal and 36,400 for harm). The correction and the refit use the same labels: those of the training budget and of 4,000 estimation prompt groups, so both are counted with the training budget plus 4,000. The change from a starting probe alone to its corrected probe is therefore what the labels add on top of the map, not a comparison at an equal number of labels; the comparison at equal labels is the one with the refit.
  • What the numbers are. Means over 8 seeds; a change is taken within a seed, on that seed's test set (about 24,200 test prompts for refusal and 58,000 test prompts for harm). Two 95% intervals are given: a paired-t interval over the 8 seeds, which covers other draws of the training and estimation prompt groups, and the interval of a bootstrap over test prompt groups (2,000 replicates, fitted probes held fixed), which covers the sampling of the test set. Neither covers both, and neither covers other checkpoints or layers. At the three training budgets, the 12 changes on top of the two Procrustes maps are positive in all 8 seeds in 12 of 12 cases, and both intervals exclude zero in 12.
  • Scale. The headroom, full refit minus frozen probe, is 45.2 points of TPR at 1% FPR for refusal (41.6% against 86.8%) and 4.4 points for harm (67.2% against 71.5%).

What these numbers rest on, and what they cannot show

  • 96 of 96 planned cells were computed; a cell is one transfer at one training budget and one seed, and every probe of a cell shares its training prompts, its estimation set and its test set, which no fit and no selection step sees. No cell and no probe was skipped.
  • Every number is a mean over 8 seeds. A seed redraws the training prompt groups and the estimation set; two seeds share about 85% of their test prompt groups on refusal and 75% of their test prompt groups on harm, so the 8 seeds are not 8 independent experiments and an interval over seeds does not contain test-set sampling error. The bootstrap over test prompt groups holds the fitted probes fixed. No interval covers both.
  • Check against the paper's stored values: in 23 comparisons, all 1,072 recomputed per-seed values equal the stored ones in every digit. Check against an earlier stored run of Procrustes followed by the correction on harm, which the paper does not report: in 10 of the 13 comparisons that are expected to match, all 352 recomputed per-seed values equal the stored ones in every digit; in the other three, for which that run had fitted the same model with its rows in another order, 117 of 144 values are equal in every digit and the largest difference is 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ AUROC and 0.09 points of TPR, inside the tolerance of the design. In that run the correction had started from a Procrustes map fitted on half of the training prompt groups, so two further comparisons with it differ by design.
  • Scope: two transfers, one layer, one model family and one size of the estimation set (4,000 prompt groups). How the comparison behaves with a smaller estimation set (R1-E1 varies its size for the correction on the frozen probe only) or on other transfers was not measured.
  • The final text of the report and its figure were written after the verification and were not checked again by a second independent verification.
