Fighting the Drift: probe reuse and orthogonal correction

Four follow-up experiments on reusing an old probe after further training of the model, and on the paper's orthogonal correction (F1 to F4 here; experiments 1 to 4 of the request). Each finding is a chart with the finding as its title; every chart has a table view, and definitions, methods, full tables and the request open on demand. All 4 experiments are final and independently verified.

Terms used on this page
Old probe, new probe:
the old probe was trained on an earlier checkpoint of the model (here Base); the new probe is fitted on labelled prompt groups of the later checkpoint (its fitting groups). Their outputs on a prompt are the old score and the new score. The request calls them the prior and the target.
TPR at 1% FPR:
share of positive test rows scored above the threshold that 1% of negative test rows exceed; the page's measure of detection. Differences are in percentage points.
Prompt group:
prompts that share their first eight normalised words; groups, not prompts, are assigned to fitting, estimation or test.
Estimation set:
labelled prompt groups of the later checkpoint that are set aside to estimate the overlap and to tune weights; they are never test rows.
Seed:
one of 8 random draws; each has its own old probe and its own labelled prompt groups. What else a seed fixes is said in the definitions of each experiment.
The two intervals:
"over seeds" is a 95% t interval of the 8 seed values or seed differences, with the test rows fixed; "test sampling" is a 95% interval from resampling whole test prompt groups, with the probes fixed. A third, "combined", adds both and is approximate.
Resolved:
all three intervals exclude 0 on the same side. "Unadjusted" means that no correction for the number of comparisons was applied.
Diagnostic:
an experiment or a comparison that is reported with all its intervals and is not the basis of a significance claim; its comparisons are not adjusted for their number.
Descriptive:
a comparison that was not among those fixed before the run; it is shown for context.
Setting, run, case:
in F3 a setting is one transfer at one fitting size and one weight (12 in all, 6 per transfer); a run is one transfer at one fitting size and one seed (32); a case is one seed at one setting.
Cell, prompt format:
in F2 a cell is one checkpoint (its weights) run on one prompt format; a prompt format is the text around the prompt and the token that the probe reads (the request calls it a rendering). A native cell is a checkpoint on its own format, a crossed cell a checkpoint on the other one.
Labels fixed:
in F2 every cell is scored against the same refusal labels, taken from one checkpoint's own responses; no response is generated for a crossed cell.
Native-format change, match effect:
in F2 the native-format change is D − A: weights and prompt format both change, as in Figure 2 of the paper, but under common labels. The match effect is (D − B) − (C − A): how much more the two native cells detect than the two crossed cells.
Stored activations, new extraction:
in F2 the stored activations are those behind Figure 2 of the paper; the new extraction is the set of activations extracted for this experiment, with another inference engine.
Labelled groups, cell:
in F1 the number of distinct prompt groups of the new checkpoint whose labels any fitting or tuning step reads (100 or 500; 250 and 1,000 are diagnostic); a cell is one transfer at one such number.
Refit; correction, cross-fitted; signed direct blend:
in F1 and F4 a refit is a new probe fitted on all the labelled groups, cross-validated. The correction, cross-fitted, is the old probe's score plus a tuned weight times the refit's score with its overlap with the old score removed, tuned on out-of-fold scores so that every label also trains the probe. The signed direct blend is a weighted sum of the two scores, either sign, tuned on the same scores.
Frozen probe, old probe unchanged:
both use zero labels of the new checkpoint. The frozen probe is the old probe exactly as it was trained; the old probe unchanged is its direction applied to the new checkpoint's standardised activations, where the correction starts.
Published correction, matched split refit:
the paper's procedure: one part of the labelled groups fits the new probe, the other estimates the correction's overlap and weight, or tunes the refit.
Standardisation:
in F1 and F4, how the new checkpoint's activations are centred and scaled: with the mean and SD of the labelled groups themselves (in-split), or of 20,000 unlabelled prompt groups (the paper's).
Resolved after adjustment:
in F1 the eight primary comparisons are called resolved only when, in addition, their Holm-adjusted p-values over the four cells of the comparison are below 0.05.
Observed frontier:
in F1 a point is on it when no other measured point uses at most as many labels and detects at least as much, one of the two strictly.
Archive:
in F4 the 4,000 labelled prompt groups of the new checkpoint that already exist and that every method may use.
F1 to F4:
this page's names for the request's experiments 1 to 4, in the request's order.

Definitions and method
  • Data. OLMo-3 7B, refusal labels, layer 16, the activation at the last token of the prompt, linear probes, 8 seeds. Primary transfers: Base to Instruct-SFT and Think-SFT to Think-DPO; four more transfers are diagnostic. One fixed test set for all seeds, label counts and methods: 23,577 prompts (8,273 refusals for Base to Instruct-SFT, 6,320 for Think-SFT to Think-DPO), after removing prompts whose text, with an instruction wrapper removed, also occurs outside the test set.
  • Labelled groups. The number of distinct prompt groups of the new checkpoint whose labels any fitting or tuning step reads: 100 and 500 (fixed before the run), 250 and 1,000 (diagnostic). They are drawn without looking at labels, from prompts that the old probe was not trained on (103 to 145 prompts at 100 groups, 519 to 578 at 500, on the two primary transfers). A seed fixes the old probe and the draw of labelled groups.
  • Frozen probe: the old checkpoint's probe with its original standardisation, zero new labels. Old probe unchanged: the old probe's direction applied to the new checkpoint's standardised activations, zero new labels; the correction starts from it. Refit on 20,000 labelled groups: reference only.
  • Standardisation of the new checkpoint's activations. In-split (primary for the methods that train on all labels): mean and SD of the labelled groups that the fit itself uses. The paper's (primary for the published split): label-free mean and SD of 20,000 prompt groups. Every method was run in both; a comparison always holds it fixed.
  • Refit: a logistic probe fitted on all the labelled groups, regularisation chosen by five-fold cross-validation over groups.
  • Correction, cross-fitted (a new variant of the paper's Equation 5, not the published method): the old probe's score plus a weight times the refit's score with its within-class slope on the old score removed. Slope and weight are chosen on out-of-fold scores of the same groups, the weight by the paper's bounded search; the final new direction is the refit's, so every group trains it.
  • Signed direct blend: cos(t) times the old score plus sin(t) times the refit's score, with t chosen among 1,002 angles from 0 to 180 degrees on the same out-of-fold scores; beyond 90 degrees the coefficient on the old score is negative.
  • Published correction and matched split refit: the paper's procedure on two halves of the labelled groups. One half fits the new probe; the other estimates slope and weight, or tunes the refit's regularisation. The paper's skip rules are recorded and not enforced; one more variant enforces them.
  • Detection. TPR at 1% FPR: the threshold is the 99th percentile of the scores of the test prompts that are not refused, and TPR is the share of refused test prompts strictly above it (realised FPR 0.997% to 1.000%, 153 to 172 false positives, on the two primary transfers). It is a point of the test ROC curve, not a deployed threshold. AUROC is the secondary measure.
  • Intervals. Over seeds: paired t interval of the 8 seed differences (for a level, t interval of the 8 seed values); it covers the old probe and the draw of labelled groups. Test sampling: 2,000 bootstrap draws of whole test prompt groups, one draw shared by all methods and seeds, with the fitted probes fixed and the threshold recomputed in each draw. Combined: both standard errors added in quadrature, with the t quantile of the seeds (approximate).
  • Resolved: the 95% interval over seeds, the 95% test-sampling interval and the combined 95% interval all exclude 0 on the same side. For the eight primary comparisons of F1 (correction minus refit and correction minus blend in the four cells) the three Holm-adjusted p-values over the four cells of the comparison must also be below 0.05; every other difference is marked unadjusted: it is not adjusted for the number of comparisons. The same eight comparisons with the paper's standardisation are the secondary set; it is adjusted within itself.
  • “Perform similarly” is used for the correction against the blend only, when the difference is unresolved and all three intervals lie within 1.0 point of 0; the margin was fixed before the run.

What this experiment cannot show (transfers chosen after Figure 19; test prompts not fresh; a new variant; no harm)
  • One model family (OLMo-3 7B), one layer, the last token of the prompt, linear probes. TPR at 1% FPR is a point of the test ROC curve, not a deployed threshold. The test set is filtered for exact repeats of a prompt after wrapper removal only; near-duplicates remain.
  • The two primary transfers were chosen after Figure 19 of the paper had been seen; they are not a random sample of transfers. The other four are diagnostic, and the six share prompts, label draws and test prompts.
  • The test prompts are not fresh: the fixed test set is the one on which Figure 19's values of one seed were computed, and most of every other seed's Figure 19 test prompts are in it. No unused test prompts exist in this pool.
  • The eight seeds share one test set and one pool of prompts: the interval over seeds covers the old probe and the draw of labelled groups, not a new test sample.
  • The correction, cross-fitted, is a new variant. Its result says nothing about the published procedure, which at 100 groups runs with its skip rule recorded and not enforced.
  • In-split standardisation rests on few prompts at 100 groups (103 to 145 labelled prompts in all, and fewer inside each fold); the paper's standardisation reads unlabelled activations of 20,000 prompt groups and is another quantity.
  • In one seed a single prompt group holds 24.8% of the labelled prompts at 100 groups (6.2% at 500).
  • Labels are drawn from prompts that the old probe was not trained on; no label count below 100 groups was run; nothing here bears on harm.
  • Correction minus refit alone cannot tell a gain of the correction step from an old probe that is better than a small refit when it is used unchanged; correction minus blend compares a bounded search with a grid search. The parts shown in Findings 1, 2 and 4 are needed for either reading.
  • The results do not show that orthogonalisation adds detection over a tuned direct blend, and they do not show that it does not: no difference between the two is resolved.
How the numbers were checked
  • No cell was aborted and no method is without a score: 224 of 224 planned cells were written.
  • Checks before the runs, all passed but one: the labelled sets and the test set were rebuilt from the prompts; 16 saved cells of round 1 and the stored values behind the paper's panels at 100 and 500 fitting groups (96 cells on six transfers) were reproduced with the new code; the bootstrap arithmetic was checked against explicitly repeated rows; a repeated run gave identical files.
  • One check before the runs failed as written, and the runs were started with that recorded. The new code's search for the correction's weight was compared with the published search on 8 saved cases. In 1 of the 8 the two searches stopped at slightly different weights, so that the AUROC they reach on the estimation set differs by 0.00000468 where the limit was 0.000001. In the other 7 the limit is met, and in all 8 the overlap estimate, the residual direction and the scoring are identical. The check stays recorded as failed; its limit was not changed, and the arithmetic fixed before the run was used unchanged. The decision to run was taken before any cell of the full runs existed.
  • How far that search's result moves under a differently rounded AUROC (a read-out added after the design; no rule uses it): repeating the search with an AUROC computed exactly from rank sums moves one seed's TPR by at most 1.77 points for the correction, cross-fitted, and 4.21 for the published correction, both at 100 groups outside the four primary cells; in the four primary cells by at most 0.02 and 1.45 points. A per-seed value of these two methods, and the search-rule part of Finding 4, are not read more finely than this (full tables).
  • Test values seen early: while a helper that prints result rows was being tested, test-set values of the 16 cells that existed then were printed, before the decision to run and before the full runs: one or two seeds of each of the four primary cells of F1 and six cells of F4, without an interval. No rule, grid, set or statistic was changed afterwards, and these cells were fitted again in the full runs.
  • After the full runs an independent verification with separate code recomputed every row of the result tables (13,664 differences) and every measure of every method from the saved scores, and refitted 18 of the 224 cells from the activations; no number disagreed. Its findings concerned wording. The other cells were not refitted (their saved arrays and measures were checked), and the wording changes made afterwards were checked against the tables by the authors only, not independently.
  • F4 was run by the same code and shares these checks.

Definitions and method

  • Two fixed-weight updates, at c = 0.25, 0.5 and 1 (fixed before the run): raw addition, old + c × new; residual update, old + c × (new − overlap). Same scores and same c in both; what is left after the subtraction is not rescaled.
  • References from the same run, with weights tuned on the estimation set: the paper's correction (overlap subtracted, the remaining direction normalised, its weight found by the paper's search) and the signed direct blend (a weighted sum of the two scores, the weights chosen on a grid of angles, either sign).
  • Intervals. Over seeds: paired t interval of the 8 seed differences (for a level, t interval of the 8 seed values). Test sampling: 2,000 bootstrap draws of whole test prompt groups, with the probes and every rule fixed and the threshold recomputed in each draw. Combined: both standard errors added in quadrature, with the t quantile of the seeds (approximate).

What this experiment cannot show (a fixed weight is not the paper's correction; two transfers; no adjustment for the number of comparisons)
  • A fixed-weight comparison holds the weight of the new score fixed. It cannot show an advantage over a direct blend with tuned weights, and it is not the paper's correction, which normalises what is left after the subtraction and tunes its weight. The refusal result is therefore a statement about raw addition at a fixed positive weight only.
  • The two updates differ only in the coefficient on the old probe's score, so the sign of their difference says where the best coefficient lies at that c. It does not say whether orthogonalisation as a procedure helps.
  • The overlap statistic is descriptive, and its fall is expected by construction of the estimate.
  • The weight c acts on scores scaled to SD 1 on the estimation set; another scale would give other positions for the same c. Three values of c were tested; nothing is shown for other values.
  • Two transfers, fitting groups drawn from the prompts the old probe was trained on (the paper's partition), an estimation set of 4,000 groups, 100 and 500 fitting groups. Nothing about smaller estimation sets, other transfers or other fitting sizes. A result on one transfer is not carried to the other.
  • The test rows are not fresh: round 1 computed the paper's values and the tuned searches on the same test rows. The 8 seeds draw their test rows from one pool, so the seed interval is not an interval over independent test samples.
  • One model family, one layer, the last prompt token, linear probes. TPR at 1% FPR is a point of the test ROC curve; a deployed threshold would need its own calibration data.
  • No adjustment for the number of comparisons (12 on TPR, as many on AUROC, 4 on the overlap).
  • Exploratory, added after the results had been seen: with a Bonferroni factor of 12 on the p-value of the combined interval, 9 of 10 resolved TPR differences stay below 0.05; harm, 500 fitting groups, c = 0.5 (p = 0.0072) would be 0.087.

Fixed-weight overlap check

Checkpoint pairs and budgets: OLMo-3 7B Base → Think-SFT refusal and Base → DPO harm. Reuse 100 or 500 fitting groups plus 4,000 estimation groups, seeds 0–7.

What the result tells us: Tests whether adding the shared score component hurts detection at a fixed update size. Use saved scores where available.

Testing whether the raw update overweights the prior

Suppose the target score v contains both a copy of the prior score u and a useful residual r, with v = βu + r. At a fixed positive update size c, raw addition gives (1 + cβ)u + cr. Subtracting the estimated overlap first gives u + cr. Both updates add the same residual. Comparing them tests whether changing the coefficient on the prior improves detection.

Use the saved scores from Reviewer 2

Use Base → Think-SFT refusal and Base → DPO harm, layer 16. Run m = 100 and 500 fitting groups, |E| = 4,000 estimation groups, seeds 0–7. These runs cover both concepts and the published low-m regime. Reuse stored per-example scores if their IDs and fitted probes match. If necessary, regenerate scores for those saved probes and splits. No new labels are required.

Hold the update size fixed

Estimate the overlap. For each seed and budget, put u and v on comparable scales using means and standard deviations from E only. Apply the same transforms to test scores. On E, subtract each class mean from each standardised score. Estimate β as the sum of the cross-products of these centred scores divided by the sum of squared centred prior scores. Freeze β and the scale factors.

Score both fixed updates. Prespecify c = 0.25, 0.5 and 1. At every c score u + cv and u + c(v − βu). Keep u, v, the fitted probes and c identical across the two updates. Use the same update scale throughout, without renormalising the residual separately. This diagnostic holds the residual contribution fixed. Use the published normalisation for the actual correction benchmarks.

Check overlap and detection separately. Measure the pooled within-class correlation of u with v, then of u with v − βu, on the held-out data. Centre by test class only for this descriptive statistic. Score predictions with the frozen transforms and β. Report signed correlations, their magnitudes and β. Flag zero variances. Compute paired TPR at 1% FPR and AUROC for each c, alongside the published correction and the independently tuned signed direct blend from the same run.

What would support the explanation

The residual’s pooled within-class covariance with the prior is zero on E by construction. Reduced overlap on test asks whether that estimate generalises. If β is positive, overlap falls and the residual update improves held-out detection, raw addition overweighted the prior at that c. If overlap falls and detection barely moves, the mechanism is visible but its practical benefit is unresolved. Report every c and both transfers. A fixed-weight gain alone cannot establish an advantage over the tuned direct blend.

What this experiment cannot show (one fixed archive; refusal only; an archive must exist for the new checkpoint)
  • One model family (OLMo-3 7B), one layer, the last token of the prompt, linear probes. TPR at 1% FPR is a point of the test ROC curve, not a deployed threshold.
  • One fixed archive in the primary run: how the result varies with which 4,000 groups form the archive is covered only by the run with per-seed archives.
  • The seeds share all 4,000 archived groups, so the interval over seeds covers the old probe, the folds and the new groups, not the draw of labelled groups as a whole.
  • Refusal only. For refusal an archive must be labelled at the new checkpoint itself: a prompt can be reused across checkpoints, the judgements of its responses cannot. The experiment does not show that such an archive exists in practice.
  • Harm was not measured again. On harm the labels belong to fixed exchanges and can be reused at every checkpoint, by a refit as well as by the correction, so the comparison at a matched number of labels is the relevant one there. How the numbers were checked

  • One check before the runs failed as written, and the runs were started with that recorded. The new code's search for the correction's weight was compared with the published search on 8 saved cases. In 1 of the 8 the two searches stopped at slightly different weights, so that the AUROC they reach on the estimation set differs by 0.00000468 where the limit was 0.000001. In the other 7 the limit is met, and in all 8 the overlap estimate, the residual direction and the scoring are identical. The check stays recorded as failed; its limit was not changed, and the arithmetic fixed before the run was used unchanged. The decision to run was taken before any cell of the full runs existed.
  • How far that search's result moves under a differently rounded AUROC (a read-out added after the design; no rule uses it): repeating the search with an AUROC computed exactly from rank sums moves one seed's TPR by at most 0.17 points for the correction, cross-fitted, and 0.02 for the published correction in this experiment.
