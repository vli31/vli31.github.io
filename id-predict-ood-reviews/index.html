Reviewer comments → experiments → manuscript edits

Submission 190 (Accept). Experiments run 2026-09-08 on the released artefacts: 270 Dyck-1 Transformers × 5 checkpoints, 1K ID / 1K OOD test strings, 79 question-formation (QF) models. Recomputed ID quantities match the released CSVs. AUROC = predicting hierarchical generalisation (OOD acc ≥ 0.5) of held-out models; chance 0.50. Numbers from results/; figures below the table.

Reviewer (exact quote) Experiment and result Edit for the final manuscript
RAoL W1. "The paper defines/presents hierarchical heads using whether attention tracks violations of the very NESTED constraint that defines the OOD distinction, which makes the predictor highly task-specific and somewhat close to constructing a detector for the target property. A stronger demonstration would be needed to show that internal analysis can predict an OOD behavior whose relevant computational property was not explicitly used to define the interpretability criterion." E1 (Fig. E1), E4 (Fig. E4). Built ID features with no reference to depth or violations: attention entropy, attention to BOS/self/first/last token, positional profile, all-query statistics; and the RDM of the final-token representation. Fit predictors on some models, tested on held-out models. Dyck-1 AUROC: generic attention 0.89–0.94, representational geometry 0.89–0.91, paper's fixed rule 0.88, paper's head scores 0.89–0.90. Generic features predict the paper's head scores with CV R² 0.84–0.86. QF (n=79): generic 0.84, matrix-auxiliary feature 0.75. Add subsection "Task-agnostic predictors" with the AUROC table; state that the hierarchical-head criterion is one instance of a general ID→OOD signal.
RAoL W2. "t-SNE is designed to preserve local neighborhoods while distorting global geometry, so apparent clusters can arise from the visualization procedure and its hyperparameters rather than from genuinely discrete structure in the models' OOD behavior, I guess it would be good to add analysis to quantify clustering directly in the original OOD-output space and test whether models genuinely form distinct behavioral modes rather than a continuous distribution that t-SNE makes look categorical." E2 (Fig. E2a, E2b). In the raw 1000-d OOD-output space: k-means silhouette 0.57 (k=2), 0.54 (k=3) vs max 0.39 / 0.21 under covariance-matched Gaussian and PCA-box uniform nulls; bootstrap ARI 0.98; dip tests reject unimodality for OOD accuracy, PC1, pairwise distances (p<0.001); gap statistic selects no k. k=3: count-matching 94 models (acc ≤0.20, silhouette 0.85), intermediate 59 (acc 0.20–0.57, silhouette 0.14), hierarchical 117 (acc 0.61–1.0, silhouette 0.50). 57% of models have OOD acc in (0.1, 0.9). PC1 = 72% of variance, Spearman 0.99 with OOD acc. Isolated t-SNE blob = 14 first-symbol models. Intermediate models' per-example decisions are predictable from string features (+0.25 over majority). Replace "models fall into discrete clusters" with "two crisp modes (count-matching, first-symbol) and a graded continuum toward hierarchical; the t-SNE gradient is OOD accuracy." Add PCA panel beside t-SNE; silhouette/null/dip statistics to appendix.
RAoL. "The distinction between predictive and causal evidence is made several times in Sections 4.3 and 5, and could be reduced to make it concise and easily readable." — State once at the start of 4.3; one back-reference in 5.
RAoL. "Instead of framing the work broadly as a 'new mission for interpretability,' (in the intro), a more concrete statement could be added somethiing like 'we introduce OOD behavioral prediction from ID internal states as an additional evaluation objective for interpretability'." — Use the reviewer's sentence in the intro; remove "mission" from abstract and conclusion.
tsfC W1. "One thing it would be nice to have seen is more connection to explanations in literature that evaluate interpretability methods by how well they predict OOD. ... LIME and SHAP ... simulatability ... (see Hase and Bansal, 2020) ... Hase et al. 2020, Mayne et al. 2026. ... it would be useful to tie it to the theoretical idea of prediction as an evaluation method, which is a long-established idea." — Related work paragraph: prediction as an evaluation criterion is simulatability (Hase & Bansal 2020; Hase et al. 2020; Mayne et al. 2026) and underlies LIME/SHAP; this work differs in reading internal states rather than input–output pairs or explanations, and in evaluating on OOD inputs where ID-equivalent models diverge. Cite where the objective is introduced.
tsfC W2. "it seemed that it was based on analysing the different attention patterns from the two groups of trained models. ... If this is the case, it seems quite circular. i.e. the two groups are established based on their OOD performance, then differences in the attention patterns are associated with the different generalisations. ... Perhaps one change to the methodology could be to train another model to predict the generalisation based on ~150 of the transformer models and show that this generalises to the ~100 held-out models." E1 (Fig. E1). 50 random splits, 150 train / 120 test: logistic regression on the paper's ID head scores, accuracy 0.82, AUROC 0.88; paper's fixed rule 0.79 / 0.88; random forest 0.89; majority 0.52. Baselines: hyperparameters alone (n_layer, n_head, wd) 0.88–0.90; the 1000 ID output probabilities alone 0.93. Leave-one-(n_layer, wd)-cell-out: hyperparameters 0.44–0.49; ID outputs 0.82; RSA k-NN 0.80; paper rule 0.74; head scores 0.73. Leave-one-seed-out: all ≈0.9. Add "Held-out prediction" to Section 4 with the 150/120 table and both baselines; make the leave-one-cell-out result the evidence that internals predict beyond training configuration.
tsfC. "More explanation about how you do the classification after clustering given there are 270 models." — Methods: head type is assigned from ID attention only (score ≥0.8 on any head in any layer); report counts per type [from Fig. 5]; t-SNE is visualisation only and never enters classification.
DaQT W1. "Novelty is overstated. The paper frames predictive interpretability as a new paradigm, but the distinction between diagnostic/predictive usefulness and causal responsibility is already well established." — Remove "new paradigm"/"new mission". Describe the contribution as an empirical demonstration in populations of ID-equivalent models, including a case where prediction and ablation disagree. Cite simulatability work.
DaQT W2. "The proposed framework is not sufficiently general. The experiments assume known candidate behaviors and hand-motivated internal features that correspond to those behaviors. It is unclear how the approach would work when the relevant strategies or OOD failure modes are not known in advance." / "Please explain how the framework would extend to settings where the relevant OOD behaviors or candidate algorithms are not known in advance." E3 (Fig. E3), negative. Unsupervised clustering of ID internals recovers architecture (ARI 1.0 with n_layer), not the rule (ARI 0.24–0.28); layer-agnostic features ARI ≈0; RSA-geometry clustering degenerate. Signal exists but is not dominant: nearest ID-geometry neighbours differ in OOD acc by 0.18 vs 0.42 for random pairs. E1. With OOD labels for a subset, task-agnostic features transfer to unseen models (0.89–0.94), unseen hyperparameter cells (0.73–0.80), earlier checkpoints (E5). Add a limitation: mode discovery without labelled models or candidate behaviours failed in this population; the approach needs a small labelled subset or hypothesised behaviours. Given either, task-specific features are not needed (point to the task-agnostic subsection).
DaQT W3. "The population-level methodology limits applicability. The predictive analysis relies on many independently trained models, whereas realistic interpretability settings may involve only one or a few expensive models." E1, E5 (Fig. E5). Predictor transfers without retraining: leave-one-seed-out ≈0.9; leave-one-cell-out 0.73–0.80. ID internals at 200K datapoints predict the 1M rule with AUROC 0.89 (paper head scores 0.89, generic 0.89, hyperparameters 0.88); the model's own OOD acc at 200K also gives 0.89. 16% of models change class after 200K, 6% after 600K. State the limitation, then: "The population is needed to fit the predictor, not to apply it; once fit, it transfers to new models and earlier checkpoints." No single-model claim.
DaQT W4. "The ablation results may be overinterpreted. A mismatch between correlation and ablation does not necessarily imply that causal interpretation is misleading; it may also reflect limitations of the intervention itself, including redundancy, compensation, or off-distribution internal states." — (QF targeted ablations already in paper: mean ΔOOD −0.007 to +0.002.) Add to 4.3: a mismatch can arise from redundancy, compensation or off-distribution internal states; we make no claim about which; predictive value is assessable without resolving it.
DaQT W5. "The experiments are highly controlled. The synthetic tasks are useful as a proof of concept, but the paper sometimes draws broader conclusions than these settings seem to justify." — Scope general claims to "populations of small Transformers on tasks with ID-equivalent rules"; move expectations to Future Work.
DaQT. "Statements overclaiming the novelty of the perspective need to be toned down." — Covered by the W1 edit.
DaQT. "The question-formation population is reported as 73 models in the main text and 79 models in Appendix M; please reconcile this inconsistency." E4. Released CSVs: 79 models, 79 distinct seeds (42–120), all ID acc 1.0, so no accuracy filter removes any. Drive folder: 119 run directories, 40 duplicate-seed re-runs dropped by the notebook (first directory per seed) → 79. Change 73 to 79 in the main text; Appendix M is correct.
DaQT. "line 1517 'is not a generic but be present a proof of existence'" — "is not a general result but a proof of existence."
(Not raised by reviewers.) E1. k-NN in generic ID-feature space predicts a held-out model's 1000 OOD outputs with 0.86 per-example agreement; population-majority vector 0.64; class oracle 0.88. Intermediate models: 0.82 vs 0.74. One sentence in Section 4 noting prediction extends to individual OOD inputs; numbers in appendix.
